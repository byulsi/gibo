# Research framing

Start with the user's objective, investment universe or instrument, horizon,
point-in-time context, analytical mode, requested output, and memory intent.
These dimensions frame the work; they do not select a fixed asset-specific
workflow. A current document or dataset supplied by the user is external
evidence for task routing even when it is stored inside the project. Reuse
adequate project evidence before collecting more.

That routing rule applies to ordinary source files outside `guru-maker/`. A
retained Evidence record under `guru-maker/` is project memory and must be
retrieved through the Runtime even when it is current or originated from the
user.

Evidence is adequate only when its claim scope, period, and currentness fit the
question. When new research is needed, prefer authoritative, fit-for-purpose
provenance; use primary sources when they improve accuracy, and retain a source
locator plus the relevant observation or data cutoff for material claims. A
stale element may supply context but is not a current fact.

Keep the layers distinct:

- A Wiki preserves stable descriptive context, conventions, and domain
  knowledge.
- A Lens supplies an interpretation, heuristic, or hypothesis with scope and
  limits.
- Evidence records a direct observation or reproducible derived result with
  provenance and a time or data window.
- A Method explains a repeatable procedure, inputs, parameters, validation, and
  limits.
- A Decision records a judgment made under stated information and constraints.

Wiki, Lens, and Method elements are independently understandable and usable
without structured memory relationships. Evidence and Decisions own the
minimal point-in-time relationships defined by the record contract.

**Your past self is evidence, not authority.** For a material judgment,
remember the earlier reasoning, re-test its assumptions and invalidation
conditions against current conditions, then revise the conclusion. Distinguish
a reusable principle from a historical pattern that merely looks similar.

Use validation appropriate to the work:

- verify provenance, cutoff, units, conventions, and scope for observations;
- test alternative explanations and disconfirming evidence for causal or thesis
  claims;
- state venue, timeframe, price construction, parameters, and regime
  sensitivity for technical work;
- check data lineage, leakage, out-of-sample behavior, robustness, benchmark,
  and costs for quantitative work; and
- test constraints, exposures, liquidity, funding, and scenarios for
  portfolio, risk, or relative-value work.

Apply only the checks material to the request. State consequential assumptions,
uncertainty, counterevidence, invalidation conditions, and evidence gaps.
